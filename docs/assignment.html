Why redesign this way?

The central question is not simply whether AI was involved. It is whether the assessment provides defensible evidence of what the student knows, can do, can explain, and can transfer.

“Any AI use is cheating.”

AI can support very different activities: brainstorming, proofreading, critique, debugging, generating complete answers, or practicing with hints. Set boundaries according to the learning purpose of the task.

Academic Integrity

“A finished product proves learning.”

Assisted performance and independent capability are not the same. Process evidence and brief transfer or explanation can strengthen the interpretation of a grade.

Assessment Validity

Cognitive offloading and over-reliance

Offloading is not automatically harmful. The concern is when AI replaces cognitive work students need to practice or when students stop evaluating the output.

Think first → use AI second → reflect and verify.

Cognitive Offloading

“Confident AI is probably right.”

Fluent output can still be inaccurate. Teach students to calibrate trust to evidence, stakes, and verifiability.

AI proposes; the learner verifies.

Trust Calibration

“AI detectors prove authorship.”

Detector output should not be treated as proof. Use assignment rules, process evidence, source checking, student explanation, and ordinary institutional procedures.

Proctoring is a narrow tool

Honorlock may be appropriate when secure individual performance is necessary to the construct. It should not substitute for clear expectations, thoughtful assessment design, accessible alternatives, or instructional support.

Accessibility checklist

If a submission raises concern
  1. Re-read the exact assignment-level AI and collaboration rule.
  2. Review evidence already required of all students.
  3. Check sources, calculations, methods, or reasoning where relevant.
  4. Give the student an opportunity to explain consequential decisions.
  5. Treat detector output, if used at all, as corroborating information rather than proof.
  6. Follow institutional procedures and due-process requirements.
