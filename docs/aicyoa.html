    Source-informed design notes
    • Uses scenario-based learning so students make choices and see consequences, matching the module design guidance for practical ethical judgment.
    • Frames AI as co-pilot versus shortcut or ghostwriter, with consequences tied to cognitive offloading, desirable difficulty, writing-as-thinking, and illusion of mastery.
    • Prompts declarative, procedural, and conditional thinking: what AI can do, how it is being used, and when or why that use is appropriate.
    • Uses short, mobile-friendly screens, plain language, formative feedback, and reflective prompts for community college learners.
    • Includes performance-style choices and reflection because the meta-analysis found AI literacy interventions vary by context and should move beyond recall toward skills, ethics, attitudes, and reflective application.
